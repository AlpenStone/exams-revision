## Causes of WW1

### Long-term causes of WW1 (M.A.I.N.)
**Militarism**: Countries built up large armies and navies, believing they needed strong military power to protect themselves or look powerful.​

**Alliances**: Countries formed partnerships to protect each other. This meant if one country went to war, its allies could be pulled in too.​

**Imperialism**: European countries competed to build large empires, especially in Africa and Asia. This created tension and rivalry.

**Nationalism**: Strong pride in one’s nation or ethnic group. Sometimes this turned aggressive.

## The July Crisis
- A tense period where a series of political and military decisions made by European powers led to the outbreak of WW1.
- It began with the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand.
- In response, Austria-Hungary sent an ultimatum to Serbia. When they didn't comply, Austria-Hungary declared war.
- Chain reaction triggered: Russia mobilised its army to support Serbia. Germany declared war on Russia. France mobilised its army to support Russia. Germany declared war on France and invaded Belgium to reach France. Britain declared war on Germany to defend Belgian neutrality.

Specific Sequence of Events:
- July 5th 1914: Germany gave Austria-Hungary a "blank cheque" - promising unconditional support in case of war.
- July 23rd 1914: Austria-Hungary delivers its ultimatum to Serbia.
- July 28th 1914: Austria-Hungary mobilises its army and declares war on Serbia.
- August 1st 1914: Germany mobilises its army, and declares war on Russia.
- August 3rd 1914: Germany declares war on France and invades Belgium.
- August 5th 1914: Britain declares war on Germany. 

### The Schlieffen Plan
- Designed by Alfred von Schlieffen in 1905
- Its objective was to avoid a 2 front war with France and Russia.
- Strategy: quickly move France then move troops east to fight Russia

Key points:
- Invade France through Belgium
- Sweep around Paris and force a French surrender

Outcomes:
- Invading Belgium dragged UK into the war.
- Belgians resisted long enough for France and Russia time to mobilise.
- Western front created - Germans and French 'dug-in' (trenches)
- Germany fought on 2 fronts

## End of the WW1

### How Wars could end

Surrender: One side in the conflict surrenders, usually after suffering significant losses or realising they cannot win the war.

Negotiated settlement: The warring parties may negotiate a settlement or peace agreement that outlines the end of the conflict e.g. transfer of territory, reparations.

Ceasefire: Temporary halt to fighting, often with the intention to negotiate a permanent settlement. Can be decalred unliaterlly by one side or agreed iupon by both.

Intervention of 3rd parties: Sometimes, outside actors, may intervene in a conflict to help bring it to an end.

Exhaustion: Both sides may become exhausted by the fighting and choose to end the conflict with no clear winner.

Decesivie military action: One side may achieve a clear and decisive military victory over the other, which forces the defeated party to accept the terms of the victor.

Paris Peace Conference
- Germany had surrendered in 11/11/1918. 
- Leaders from 32 countries met in Versailles, in January 1919 to talk peace.
- On 28th June 1919, the Treaty of Versailles was signed.
- Nobody from Germany was allowed to attend
- The 3 most important people at the conference was known as the Big Three:
  - Woodrow Wilson (USA)
  - David Lloyd George (UK)
  - Georges Clemenceau (France)

#### The Big Three

**Woodrow Wilson (USA)**
- Did not want to damage Germany too much
- He made up the 14 points, which he thought the countries could stick in order to make peace.
- Wnated to set up a League of Nations which ountries can join to not go to war again.

**David Lloyd George (UK)**
- Didn't want Germany to be treated too badly
- He thought this could lead to another war 
- Most British people wanted the Germans dealt with very harshly

**Georges Clemenceau (France)**
- Wanted to totally destroy Germany
- Wanted Germany to lose all their power
- Wanted Alsace-Lorraine back
- Wanted Germany to pay for all damages they had caused

### What actually happened
1. Germany lost Alsace-Lorraine
2. Germany had to demilitarise the Rhineland (the border region with France)
3. Let the League of Nations run the Saarland for 15 years and give France all its coal.
4. Lost the Polish corridor to Poland
5. Germany and Austria forbidden from joining together
6. Germany had to drastically cut down on her armed forces (100,000 men)
7. The League of Nations ran the German colonies
8. Germany had to pay 6 billion pounds in reparations
9. Germany signed a war guilt clause accepting all blame for starting the war
10. Germany cannot join the League of Nations until they were proven to be peaceful

## League of Nations
- Founded in Geneva, Switzerland in 1920
- Brainchild of Woodrow Wilson
- Aims:
  - Prevent war
  - Disarmament
  - Solve disputes through diplomacy
  - Improve global welfare
- Open to any nation except for Germany and USSR
- USA did not join
- 48 members joined in 1920, 54 by 1929
- Tried to resolve disputes through discussion, if not, it could use economic sanctions or military force using member state armies if it chose to do so
- German and Ottoman colonies were transferred to the League, but the British and French were entrusted with running them.

Structure of the League of Nations:
- Council: 4 permanent members, 5 after 1926 (Britain, France, Germany, Italy, and Japan)
  - Dealt with problems when the assembly wasn't in session. Could use sanctions.
  - Unanimous decisions made the council very slow to act. 
- Assembly
  - Debating chamber
  - Met once a year
  - Unanimous decisions
- Secretariat
  - Administration
  - Prepared reports
  - Based in Geneva
  - International Civil Service
- International Labour Organisation (ILO)
  - To improve working conditions
- Permanent Court of Justice
  - 15 judges representing world's different legal systems
  - Based in Hague, Netherlands
  - Made decisions when cases referred to it by assembly or by member countries

Failures of the League
- Japan invaded Manchuria in 1932. The League objected but could do nothing. When the League supported China, Japan left the League.
- Hitler announced that Germany was leaving the League in 1933.
- Italy invaded Abyssinia in 1935. Although the League officially condemned the Italians, France and Britain were caught making a secret agreement to give Abyssinia to Italy.

> These crisis destroyed the authority of the League, and it was powerless to stop Germany after 1935.


Manchurian Crisis:
- Japan's main export was silk but the impact of the Great Depression meant that exporting silk was to richer countries such as America dropped the price of luxury goods.  
- Japanese troops invaded Manchuria in 1931 because of an incident at the Mukden railway
- The League condemned the actions but could do nothing to remove the Japanese troops
- Japan left the League in 1933

Abyssinia Crisis:
- Italy invaded Abyssinia in 1935 after a border clash (Wal-Wal border clash)
- Caputred Addis-Ababa and the emperor (Haile Selassie) into exile
- The League condemned the actions but could do nothing to remove the Italian troops
- League imposed sanctions but they didn't include key items such as oil or coal.
- League banned loans to Italy and restrictions to trade
- Italy left the League in 1937

Rise of Nazism:
- Germany suffered huge economic difficulties - the Great Depression did not make it better
- In 1935, Hitler announced a rearmament.
- League of Nations condemned these actions but took no serious military response or punishment.
- Due to Britain and France fearing another major war no siginificant actions were taken, allowing Germany to continue their aggression and expansion.

Limitations of the League:
- Lack of military power: no army of its own, so it depended on member states to enforce decisions
- Self-interest of major powers: Britain and France were concerned about their own national interests
- Required all decisions in the League to be unanimous, which meant any nation involved could effectively block the League of Nations from taking action.
